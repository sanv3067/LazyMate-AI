# Sample Notes: Differential Equations

Differential equations describe relationships involving an unknown function and its derivatives. They are used to model changing quantities.

Newton's law of cooling states that the rate of change of an object's temperature is proportional to the difference between its temperature and the surrounding temperature.

A simple model is dT/dt = -k(T - T_s), where T is the object's temperature, T_s is the surrounding temperature, and k is a positive constant.

Population growth can be modeled by dP/dt = kP when the growth rate is proportional to population size. Here P is population and k is the growth constant.
